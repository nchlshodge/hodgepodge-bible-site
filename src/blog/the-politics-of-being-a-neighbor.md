"Who is my neighbor?" is a question people ask when they're hoping the answer is small.

The lawyer in Luke 10 asks it "wanting to justify himself" — he's not looking for truth, he's looking for a boundary he can stand safely inside of. Jesus answers with a story that erases the boundary entirely: the hero is the person from the group his audience trusted least, and the neighbor turns out to be whoever is bleeding in the ditch in front of you.

That's an uncomfortable answer regardless of which decade you're reading it in. It doesn't map cleanly onto any modern political coalition, which is probably the point — Jesus isn't handing out a platform, he's handing out an obligation.

The question worth carrying into this week's headlines isn't "which side is right." It's the lawyer's question, asked honestly instead of defensively: who is actually in front of me, and what would it cost to treat them like the story says to?
