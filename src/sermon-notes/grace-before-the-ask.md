Every command in Ephesians shows up after chapter 2, not before it. That ordering is the sermon.

**The structure that matters:** Paul spends chapters 1–3 describing what God has already done, and doesn't get to "therefore, walk" until chapter 4. Grace isn't the reward for good behavior in this letter — it's the foundation behavior gets built on.

**The phrase worth memorizing:** "by grace you have been saved, through faith, and this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God" (v.8). Notice what's *not* in that sentence: any mention of effort, worthiness, or timing. It's entirely someone else's initiative.

**Practically:** if grace comes before the ask, then failure doesn't move you out of relationship with God — it just means you're still living inside the same grace you started in. That changes what repentance feels like. It's a return, not a re-application.
