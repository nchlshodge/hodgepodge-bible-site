title: The Cost of Naming Sin
date: 2026-08-23
week: 31
reference: 2 Samuel 12:1–13
---
Nathan doesn't walk into the room and accuse David directly — he tells a story first, gets David's verdict on someone else's cruelty, and only then says the four hardest words in the chapter: "you are the man."

**Why the parable comes first:** David has spent roughly a year not confronting what he did. The story bypasses his defenses by asking him to judge a situation with no personal stake — and once he's pronounced judgment, he's already agreed to the standard before he realizes it applies to him.

**What's easy to miss:** David's response (v.13) is five words — "I have sinned against the Lord" — with no excuse attached. Compare that to Saul's confessions elsewhere, which are usually followed by a "but." The length of a confession isn't what matters. The absence of a "but" is.

**Sitting with it:** Nathan risked his life to tell that story to a king. Naming sin honestly — our own, or someone else's, carefully — has never been free. It wasn't free for him and it won't be free for us.
