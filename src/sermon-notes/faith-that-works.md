James isn't arguing against grace — he's arguing against a faith that never shows up anywhere except in what someone claims to believe.

**The core tension:** "faith without works is dead" sits right next to Paul's "by grace you have been saved through faith." James isn't contradicting that. He's answering a different question: not "what saves you," but "what does real faith actually look like once it exists."

**Three things worth carrying home:**

1. Belief that never changes behavior isn't the kind of faith James — or Jesus — is describing. Even demons believe correctly (v.19), and it does nothing for them.
2. Abraham's faith was "completed" by action (v.22) — not earned, completed. The works are the fruit, not the root.
3. The test case James picks is deliberately uncomfortable: a hungry, cold person in front of you, and "good wishes" instead of help (v.15-16).

**Question for the week:** where is there a gap between what I say I believe and what I've actually let it cost me?
